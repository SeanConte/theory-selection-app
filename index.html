Creatures
One creature on the board has been picked at random. Ask about its features, rule out the creatures that don't match, and name it.

No two creatures share the same combination of features, and every answer you get is true, so each question rules creatures out for certain.

There's no limit on questions, but fewer is better. A good question rules out a lot of creatures however it's answered.

Garden
You control the conditions in every plot of a garden. Find the combination that grows the tallest plant, and grow one to the target height before the days run out.

Drag across plots to select them, set their conditions, then run a day and see what grows. A plant grows faster the taller it already is, so finding the best conditions early matters.

Some conditions have one clearly best setting and others barely matter. On Medium and Hard, some settings work better in combination. Hover over a plot to see its history.

Outbreak
People in a small town are falling ill. Several explanations are on the table, and the first evidence you see will fit more than one of them. Work out which is right, then report how confident you are.

Each day, new cases show up on the town map and in the case chart. Watching costs nothing. Everything else costs investigation hours, and you have a fixed number of them.

Visiting a household tells you which well and food shop it uses and who works at the Mill. On Medium and Hard, other illnesses are going around too, and a lab test tells you whether a resident has the outbreak illness.

You can also close a well, a shop, or the Mill. If you close the source, new cases stop once people who were already infected have fallen ill.

By the deadline, you report to the town council by spreading 10 chips across the explanations to show how confident you are in each. Chips on the right answer raise your score and chips anywhere else lower it, so it pays to report your honest confidence.

How to use the garden

Select plots: Click and drag to select a region. Selected plots glow green.

Assign conditions: A popup appears after you select. Set one or more conditions and leave others as "no change" to update only those variables.

Run a day: Once every plot is set, click Run Day. Not every variable has the same shape: some have a sharp optimum, others are flatter, and some seeds include interactions.

Hover any plot to see its day-by-day history.

Report to the council

How to investigate

Next day moves time forward. New illness shows up as red dots in the houses and as bars in the case chart.

Click a house to see who lives there. Visiting it (1 hour) records which well and food shop the household uses and who works at the Mill.

Lab tests (1 hour, Medium and Hard) show whether a resident has or had the outbreak illness, even before symptoms. Some residents are ill with something else, and a test is the only way to tell.

Click a site to close it (3 hours). Exposure there stops at once, but anyone already infected will still fall ill over the next few days.

The evidence table compares illness among exposed and unexposed residents. Compare shows the residents exposed to one explanation's site and not the other's, which is where two explanations make different predictions.

Chips show your confidence in each explanation. Place all 10 before you report.
